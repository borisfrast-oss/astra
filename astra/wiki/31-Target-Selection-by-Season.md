# Chapter 31 – Target Selection by Season

# Dwarf mini + Siril 1.4.4 + GraXpert + GIMP 3.2.4 Best Practices Handbook

---

# Objective

This chapter describes which astronomical objects are particularly suitable for each season.

Visibility depends on:

- Season
- Time of day
- Location
- Altitude
- Moon phase
- Light pollution

The information refers to the European region (e.g., Austria / Central Europe).

---

# 1. Basic Rules for Object Selection

A good astronomical object meets as many criteria as possible:

```

high in the sky

↓

long visibility

↓

little interfering moonlight

↓

suitable filter

↓

sufficiently bright

```

---

# 2. Winter (December – February)

## Characteristics

Winter is the best season for many well-known Deep-Sky objects.

Advantages:

- Long nights
- Dry air
- Many bright objects

---

# M42 Orion Nebula

## Difficulty

Simple to intermediate.

## Suitable for:

- Beginners
- First Deep-Sky captures

---

Recommendation:

```

Exposure:
short + long combined

Filter:
no filter or dual-band

```

---

Special features:

Very bright core.

HDR recommended.

---

# M45 Pleiades

## Difficulty

Simple.

---

Recommendation:

```

no filter

120–180 seconds

many lights

```

---

Special feature:

The blue reflection nebula requires:

- Good sky
- Careful background processing

---

# M31 Andromeda Galaxy

## Season

Late summer to winter.

---

Recommendation:

```

180 seconds

Gain 30–35

no filter

```

---

Special features:

- Bright core
- Faint outer regions

HDR possible.

# 9. Recommendation for Dwarf mini in Vienna

Due to higher light pollution:

Particularly suitable:

```

Emission nebulae with dual-band

Bright galaxies

Star clusters

```

---

Less ideal:

- Very faint reflection nebulae
- Extremely diffuse galaxy structures

---

# 10. Planning with Moon

## Full Moon

Good for:

- Emission nebulae with dual-band
- Bright star clusters

---

Poor for:

- Reflection nebulae
- Galaxies

---

## New Moon

Best time for:

- Galaxies
- Faint nebulae
- Milky Way
